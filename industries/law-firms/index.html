Focus on your clients. Understand the business behind the practice.

Bank and card records help you see what your firm spends. They cannot, on their own, explain collections delays or profitability by client, matter, or practice area. 2ndCFO starts with recorded financial activity and helps frame those business questions. Additional billing, time, and cost records are needed for deeper analysis, with scope agreed before work begins.

Discuss your business

Reliable records, then business understanding

The product starts with bank and credit card CSV imports, retains source rows, and supports confirmed categorization rules and manual corrections. Analysis uses the records available for the selected period. Consistent categories make spending easier to investigate, but they do not make incomplete data complete. Confirm additional service work and its delivery schedule before relying on a report. The purpose is to reduce repeated digging and give you a clearer starting point for decisions while you focus on customers and operations.

Follow the path from work to payment

For an hourly practice, recorded time can sit unbilled before it becomes an invoice, and a billed amount can remain unpaid after the work is finished. Flat-fee and contingency practices have different timing and measurement needs. Start by identifying the fee arrangements and records that describe your practice rather than applying one utilization target to every firm. Potential review topics below depend on the available data and a separately confirmed service scope.

  • Review unbilled work and the age of outstanding invoices where records are available.
  • Distinguish fees billed, adjustments, and cash received.
  • Compare collections and delivery costs by practice area when cost allocation is supportable.

Separate operating finances from client funds

Client trust funds require separate handling from the firm's operating money. An operating cash review should not treat client balances as funds available for payroll or partner distributions. Discuss how your existing trust records and reconciliation responsibilities are managed. Any trust-account work requires an explicitly agreed scope with the firm; this page does not promise compliance oversight or replace guidance on professional obligations.

Make staffing and partner discussions more concrete

A busy team does not automatically mean the firm should hire. Review the mix of work, billing delays, write-downs, collection timing, and the full cost of additional capacity. For partner distributions, evaluate upcoming payroll, overhead, and expected receipts instead of relying on one strong collections week. Scenario planning can help make the tradeoffs visible without assuming every billed hour will be collected.

Prepare for a focused discovery conversation

Tell us the size of the practice, fee models, accounting and billing systems, and the financial question causing the most friction. A first review can establish whether the priority is bookkeeping, management reporting, or cash planning. Share system names and a general description through the contact form; do not submit privileged matter details, client names, or trust records there.

Illustrative example

Illustrative example only, not a customer result: If recorded hours increase but cash receipts do not, examine whether time is awaiting billing, invoices are aging, or write-downs increased. Each stage calls for a different conversation with the people responsible for billing and collections.

Common questions

Can reports show practice-area profitability?

Potentially, if billing and cost records support a consistent allocation. Start by agreeing which costs can be assigned directly and how shared overhead will be treated.

Does this include legal or trust-compliance advice?

No legal advice is implied. Any bookkeeping support involving trust records must be separately scoped, with the firm's professional responsibilities clearly understood.
