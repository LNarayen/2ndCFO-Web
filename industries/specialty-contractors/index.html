Reliable records, then business understanding

The product starts with bank and credit card CSV imports, retains source rows, and supports confirmed categorization rules and manual corrections. Analysis uses the records available for the selected period. Consistent categories make spending easier to investigate, but they do not make incomplete data complete. Confirm additional service work and its delivery schedule before relying on a report. The purpose is to reduce repeated digging and give you a clearer starting point for decisions while you focus on customers and operations.

Give job costs a consistent home

Comparing an estimate with actual costs requires a consistent way to assign labor, materials, subcontractors, and other direct costs. Missing job codes or late supplier invoices can make a project appear more profitable than it is. Establish the records needed before relying on a margin report to judge the estimating or field team. Potential review topics below depend on the available data and a separately confirmed service scope.

  • Compare estimated and recorded direct costs by job or phase where data permits.
  • Identify costs awaiting allocation and invoices received after a reporting cutoff.
  • Track the effect of approved changes separately from the original scope.
  • Review unpaid progress invoices and retainage separately from current cash.

Keep change orders and billing timing visible

Extra work can consume labor and materials before an approved change appears in billing. A job review should distinguish proposed changes, approved scope, billed amounts, and collected receipts. This avoids treating an expectation of payment as cash already available. The accounting treatment of work in progress and revenue recognition should be coordinated with the firm's accountant based on the contracts and reporting requirements.

Plan for the cash demands of the next job

A larger project may require deposits, mobilization, or a concentrated period of labor spending before the first payment. Compare that demand with obligations on current jobs and likely collection dates. Testing a delayed progress payment or a materials cost increase can reveal a financing or scheduling question while there is still time to consider options.

Illustrative example

Illustrative example only, not a customer result: A job with a strong billed margin may still have unrecorded subcontractor invoices and outstanding retainage. Reviewing both the cost completeness and collection schedule gives a more useful picture than comparing invoices issued with cash spent so far.

Common questions

Can bookkeeping alone tell me whether a job made money?

It needs job-level records as well as reconciled accounts. Consistent cost coding and complete labor and supplier records are essential for a meaningful job comparison.

Is this the same as HVAC service-business reporting?

Project contractors often focus on estimates, progress billing, changes, and retainage. Recurring service and repair businesses may need a different view of dispatch, service mix, and seasonality.
