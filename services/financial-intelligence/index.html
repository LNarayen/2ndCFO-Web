Services · 2ndCFO

Know where your money goes. Understand what to ask next.

You need more than a list of transactions to run a business. 2ndCFO combines bank and card records with analysis of recorded income, outflows, and category spending. Supported questions can point you to the underlying transactions. Explaining a margin change or an operating cause may require billing, payroll, or job records and your team’s context. The aim is a clearer next question and a more informed owner decision.

Discuss your business

Reliable records, then business understanding

The product starts with bank and credit card CSV imports, retains source rows, and supports confirmed categorization rules and manual corrections. Analysis uses the records available for the selected period. Consistent categories make spending easier to investigate, but they do not make incomplete data complete. Confirm additional service work and its delivery schedule before relying on a report. The purpose is to reduce repeated digging and give you a clearer starting point for decisions while you focus on customers and operations.

Choose measures tied to a decision

A long dashboard can obscure the issue. Start with the decisions you actually face: whether to adjust pricing, follow up on old invoices, change the mix of work, or review staffing. Then choose a small set of measures with consistent definitions. A margin comparison is only meaningful if labor and other direct costs are classified the same way in both periods. Potential review topics below depend on the available data and a separately confirmed service scope.

  • Revenue and gross margin by service line where the underlying records support it.
  • Receivables aging and collection trends to distinguish sales from receipts.
  • Payroll and operating expense trends relative to the volume of work.
  • Budget versus actual results with explanations for material differences.

Investigate a change before acting on it

An increase in revenue may reflect higher prices, more work, a different service mix, or a timing difference. A falling margin may come from overtime, materials, discounts, or incomplete cost entries. Analysis should test these explanations against source records and operating context rather than infer a cause from a chart alone. Your team's explanation of unusual events is part of the review.

Keep cash and profit in the same conversation

A profitable month can still create a cash shortage if customer payments arrive after payroll and supplier bills. Review collections, payment obligations, and the timing of large purchases alongside the income statement. This creates a useful bridge to cash flow planning without treating an accounting profit as money already available to spend.

Agree on the reporting inputs and rhythm

Useful analysis depends on reconciled books and reliable operating data. Before an engagement, discuss which systems contain invoices, labor, project costs, or time records; which comparisons matter; and how often information will be reviewed. The proposed scope should identify available measures and gaps. Do not assume every system has a direct integration or that every metric can update in real time.

Illustrative example

Illustrative example only, not a customer result: If revenue grows but gross profit barely changes, compare the contribution from each service line and check whether direct labor or materials grew faster than sales. That review helps distinguish growth worth pursuing from work that needs repricing.

Common questions

How is this different from bookkeeping?

Bookkeeping establishes the transaction records and financial statements. Financial intelligence interprets those results alongside operating information and helps identify questions and actions.

Do I need a new software platform?

That depends on your current records and reporting needs. Discuss the available systems and data first; no particular integration or platform is assumed.
