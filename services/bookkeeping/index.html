Spend less time chasing records. Know what you’re reviewing.

Late reports and scattered bank files pull you away from customers. 2ndCFO starts with imported bank and card records, consistent categories, and source transactions you can review. Use that foundation to understand spending and identify missing information. Any ongoing bookkeeping service, reconciliation, monthly close, and reporting schedule must be confirmed separately; importing transactions alone does not complete your books.

Discuss your business

Reliable records, then business understanding

The product starts with bank and credit card CSV imports, retains source rows, and supports confirmed categorization rules and manual corrections. Analysis uses the records available for the selected period. Consistent categories make spending easier to investigate, but they do not make incomplete data complete. Confirm additional service work and its delivery schedule before relying on a report. The purpose is to reduce repeated digging and give you a clearer starting point for decisions while you focus on customers and operations.

Start with records you can reconcile

A useful monthly close starts with complete source records. Bank and credit card activity needs to be matched to the ledger; transfers should not inflate revenue; loan payments need to separate principal from interest. Unexplained differences should be investigated rather than carried forward as if they were settled. Potential review topics below depend on the available data and a separately confirmed service scope.

  • Review the chart of accounts against how your business earns and spends money.
  • Reconcile bank and credit card balances and identify missing records.
  • Review receivables, payables, and transactions that need an owner's explanation.

Make the monthly reports useful

Your profit and loss statement describes performance over a period. Your balance sheet shows what the business owns and owes at a point in time. Neither is a substitute for checking cash availability. Read together, these reports can reveal unpaid invoices, growing liabilities, or expenses recorded in the wrong period. The close schedule and reporting scope should reflect the condition of your records and the information you can provide.

Separate ongoing work from cleanup

If prior months are unreconciled, opening balances are uncertain, or personal and business expenses are mixed, define the cleanup before assuming a routine monthly process will solve it. An initial review helps establish the periods involved, available documents, and questions for your tax preparer. Cleanup needs, software access, transaction volume, and reporting complexity all affect the proposed scope.

Bring the right questions to the first conversation

Bring your accounting system name, the date of your last completed reconciliation, the number of bank and card accounts, and your most recent financial statements if available. Tell us whether you need overdue records organized, ongoing monthly support, or better visibility into collections. Do not send bank credentials or confidential client records through the contact form.

Illustrative example

Illustrative example only, not a customer result: If sales rise while the bank balance falls, first check whether invoices remain unpaid, debt principal was repaid, or owners took distributions. Those movements explain different problems and should not all be called an expense increase.

Common questions

Does bookkeeping include tax filing?

Tax preparation and filing should be confirmed separately. Bookkeeping provides records that can support your tax preparer; it does not by itself establish a tax engagement.

Can I start with bookkeeping and add analysis later?

Start with imported records and supported transaction analysis. Discuss availability and scope for any monthly bookkeeping or additional analysis; a service engagement is not established by using the product.
